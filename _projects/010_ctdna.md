title: Characterizing tumor DNA in circulation using Oxford Nanopore sequencing
image: assets/images/Nanopore.jpg
---

Cancer cells shed genomic DNA into circulation, enabling "liquid biopsies" that detect and characterize a tumor without an invasive biopsy. We use Oxford Nanopore whole-genome sequencing to measure DNA sequence, methylation patterns, and fragment lengths from the same molecules.

We showed that multiple complementary genomic features can identify circulating tumor DNA (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02710-1" target="_blank">Katsman, Genome Biology 2022</a>), and used long-read sequencing to define altered fragmentation patterns linked to elevated circulating immune cell DNA in cancer (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-026-04060-8" target="_blank">Berman, Genome Biology 2026</a>).

A major benefit of Nanopore sequencing is that methylation markers can identify the cell types contributing DNA to plasma. We developed CelFiE-ISH for cell-type deconvolution (<a href="https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-024-03275-x" target="_blank">Unterman, Genome Biology 2024</a>) and are extending it to ultra-low-pass Nanopore data for tumor DNA detection (<a href="https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.15.718632" target="_blank">Katsman, bioRxiv 2026</a>).
